1Problem & Stakeholder

Turbines sometimes produce little or no power in good wind (shutdowns, curtailment, underperformance). SCADA logs carry no labels for when or why output was lost, and the manufacturer curve overstates what a real site can deliver.

Decision supported: where should the O&M team look first to recover the most clean energy?

Stakeholders Turbine operators, O&M engineers, asset owners, grid operators.

8Recommendations for the O&M Team

1 · ACT NOWAlert on zero output in good wind

Flag output at 0 for > 1 h while wind ≥ 3.5 m/s; review fault and stop logs for the 13 long stops first.

Up to 321 MWh · ~237 t CO₂ / yr*
2 · ACT NOWPareto-guided inspection list

Use the top-ranked month × direction conditions as the checklist, starting with January winds from 30–60°.

Inspection time goes where loss is highest
3 · NEEDS SITE CHECKFix underperformance

Check yaw, vane & anemometer calibration, pitch, soiling and icing (0–30° sector loses 13.9% of expected energy).

Up to 216 MWh · ~159 t CO₂ / yr*
4–5 · PLAN AHEADPre-winter readiness & site benchmark

Inspect Oct–Nov, ready spares & crews Dec–Feb; benchmark against the learned site curve and retrain yearly.

Shorter winter outages, fewer false alarms
6 · Needs more data: at ≥ 13 m/s, 21% of records sit below 3,500 kW. Match them against grid curtailment instructions to separate grid limits from turbine faults.

10Limitations & Assumptions

No fault labels: states are suspected, not confirmed faults.
Weak clustering: DBSCAN left the long stops in its “normal” cluster; rule-based states used instead.
617 MWh is a lower bound: the healthy model saw 5.5% zero-output and 17.5% underperforming records.
One turbine, 2018, Turkey: may not transfer to other years or to Malaysian sites.
Missing data: 3.9% of slots absent and counted as zero loss, so loss is understated.
ML did not beat a binned curve (194 vs 202 kW MAE); the main findings hold with either.

✓Conclusion

Existing SCADA data can steer maintenance to where it recovers the most clean energy; shortening long stops in good wind is the fastest win.
